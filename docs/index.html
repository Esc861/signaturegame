    About & credits

    Every signature here belongs to someone no longer living. This is a game about steady hands and the strange beauty of other people's handwriting.

    Scoring follows what a document examiner actually looks for. The first and best tell is line quality: a real signature is written fast and automatically, so it runs smooth and continuous, while a copy betrays itself with tremor and hesitation. Where it sits on the page is not a criterion at all — an exact positional match is evidence of tracing, not of authenticity — so a steady line a little off the mark beats a shaky one dead on it.

    The artwork comes from Wikimedia Commons, found by asking Wikidata which historical figures have a signature on file. Difficulty is measured from the signature itself — how much pen travel is packed into the space, how much it turns, how tangled it is, and how fine the pen was. A broad nib forgives a wobble; a hairline does not.

    It is played sideways, and every signature here is a wide one. That pair of decisions goes together: normalised to the same length, a wide hand fills the card and a square one has to shrink to fit its height, which made some signatures harder than others for no reason to do with the handwriting. Wide-only on a landscape card means every hand reaches you at the same size, and difficulty means what it says.
